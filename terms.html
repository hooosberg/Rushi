2. Description of service

Rushi is an iPhone and iPad application for reading Buddhist scripture, working a 108-bead mala, copying scripture, and playing meditation soundscapes. It provides the Diamond Sutra, the Heart Sutra, and their multi-language translations as a calligraphic reading experience; an interactive prayer-bead counter; a copy-practice canvas; and an offline soundscape mixer. All features run locally on your device and do not depend on any remote server.

5. Scripture sourcing and copyright

Every scripture passage in the App has been reviewed and verified as originating from a public-domain source edition:

  • The classical translators (Kumārajīva, Xuanzang, the 9-century Tibetan translation team, and so on) died over a millennium ago; their translations have long since exited the copyright term in every jurisdiction;
  • The modern foreign-language editions used (e.g. Goddard 1932 English, Walleser 1914 German) were published before the relevant public-domain cutoff dates (US 1929 cutoff, EU 70-years-pma expiry);
  • Non-English / non-Chinese editions (Tibetan from the Derge Kangyur, Sanskrit transliterations, and so on) come from digitisation projects (84000.co, Wikisource, Esukhia) that explicitly release their digital reproductions to the public domain;
  • Where minor passages were AI-assisted in translation and cross-reviewed, no copyright is asserted in the AI output layer; it is released alongside the source under CC0.

Each passage is shown in the App with: source-edition name, translator, date, source URL, copyright status, and the basis for that status. If you believe a passage's copyright status is misstated, please contact us using the address below and we will investigate immediately.
